# Eliance (Eliance Group Holdings / Eliance Holding Company, S.L.) — Spain, helicopter emergency & aerial services              Score: 66  [Watch]
As of: 2026-11-06

**What they are.** Madrid (Cuatro Vientos) aerial-services group founded 1958 (ex-Habock Aviation Group, renamed 2018): HEMS, firefighting, SAR, traffic surveillance and Airbus-accredited maintenance in Spain, Italy (EliFriulia, 100% since Jul 2025) and Croatia (EliAdriatik); group fleet "90+" to "c. 100" aircraft, 550+ employees group-wide, c. 230 in the Spanish entity that filed (PRNewswire 8 Apr 2025, https://prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/swiss-life-asset-managers-acquires-a-majority-stake-in-eliance-302423527.html; WFW 7 Jul 2025, https://www.wfw.com/press/wfw-advises-eliance-on-debt-refinancing-and-additional-loan-facility/; HeliHub 9 Oct 2026, https://www.helihub.com/2026/10/09/spanish-operator-eliance-starts-pre-bankruptcy-proceedings/). Owner: Swiss Life Asset Managers via the fund "Swiss Life Global Infrastructure Opportunities Growth II" (majority since Apr 2025, bought from Buenavista Equity Partners/Himilcon; RiverRock Brownfield Infrastructure Funds stays as minority) (PRNewswire, above; capital-riesgo.es 3 Jul 2025, https://capital-riesgo.es/en/articles/buenavista-completes-the-sale-of-its-stake-in-eliance-to-swiss-life-asset-managers/). CEO Fernando Gallardo (ex-group CFO, ex-head of Italian subsidiary) since Mar 2026, replacing Juan Carlos Martínez (HeliHub, above; AeroTime Oct 2026, https://www.aerotime.aero/articles/spanish-helicopter-operator-eliance-files-for-preliminary-creditor-protection/amp). Decision-maker is therefore an institutional fund team, not a founder.

- Deadline: a preconcurso gives 3 months (+1) to file a plan or concurso — i.e. by early Feb 2027 at the latest; if a concurso was already declared in late October, the clock is now the administrador concursal's and any investor route is a sale of productive units (venta de unidad productiva) through the court.

**What they'll need.** Estimate: EUR 15–40M of new money (rescue equity or super-senior/sub-debt) to restore operations (pilot training, maintenance, replacement aircraft) and reset the Infranity facility; or, more likely given a financial owner that has stopped funding, a sale of the Spanish operating units (and/or the Italian/Croatian subsidiaries) to a strategic or specialist operator-investor. Timing: inside 90 days. All amounts are estimates; no debt or liquidity figure beyond the EUR 190M facility and the EUR 23.9M loss is public.

**Why it's ours.** Banks will not lead: infrastructure-fund owner that has (reportedly) stopped injecting, a EUR 190M institutional senior lender, public-contract execution failures with daily fines, a live CNMC cartel probe, and a court process. Those are exactly the complications a readiness programme packages for investors who price them: restated numbers, contract-by-contract viability, a clean data room, and a structured new-money or unit-sale process. Comparable: the Mexican health-insurer recap (USD 22M from a single investor, financials restated, data room built, inside 60 days after the banks stepped back) — never name the client.

**Regulatory note.** Spain is MiFID II territory (CNMV). Placing/arranging financial instruments is a reserved investment service; corporate-finance advice on capital structure and M&A is an ancillary service that a non-licensed adviser can give. EGP's mandate must be drafted as advisory/consulting (readiness + capital-structure advice) with a success fee, with no placing, no handling of client money and no marketing of a fund interest in Spain; Spanish counsel to confirm the wording and the cross-border (Dubai → Spain) position. Process control: in a preconcurso the debtor keeps full management powers and runs its own negotiations (no administrador concursal); an experto en la reestructuración can be appointed on request but none is reported. Practically, a fund-owned company in preconcurso is run by its owner and its senior lender — so the first message must go to the owner's deal team (Swiss Life AM) via their counsel, not to the court. If the reported late-October concurso declaration is real, the debtor keeps administration under the intervention of an administrador concursal, and any investor or unit sale needs the administrador's and the court's blessing — then EGP's product is buy-side/investor sourcing for a unit sale, not a readiness programme.

**Risks.**
- Institutional owner with its own banks: Swiss Life AM and Infranity will default to Lazard/big-four/Houlihan-type advisers. EGP is a vendor to them, not a peer. Real probability that this is an exit or secondary (unit sale), not a readiness client.
- Process may already be in concurso (two reports, no court record retrieved). If so, a fixed readiness fee is hard to get paid and the timetable belongs to the administrador concursal.
- Owner appears to have stopped funding (Forbes, internal sources). An owner that will not write a cheque is also unlikely to pay EUR 35k to an unknown Dubai adviser — unless it wants a cheap route to an exit.
- Operational collapse in progress: pilots unlicensed, aircraft grounded, daily fines, re-tenders launched by Madrid and Galicia; contracts (the asset) are being lost weekly. Revenue of EUR 54M may not survive to a closing.
- CNMC cartel investigation: potential fines and reputational toxicity for any investor; also a reason for due-diligence depth we cannot pay for pre-fee.
- No off-book evidence, but the EUR 14.7M of "claims, penalties and provisions" and the Infranity covenant position are opaque.
- Workforce and public-service politics (CCOO, UGT, Interior Ministry, Comunidad de Madrid) will shape outcomes more than finance.
