# LatAm Hunter Sweep — regulated mid-market recap / restructuring candidates

Prepared 2026-11-02. Profile: regulated LatAm companies (insurers, health payers, non-bank lenders, healthcare, asset managers) with signs of needing USD 10–100M of recap / restructuring / growth equity and a reason banks and bulge-bracket advisers stay away. Reference deal (Plan Seguro, Mexico) deliberately excluded.

Method: WebSearch + WebFetch of regulator resolutions (SSN, CNBV, SBS, SUSEP, ANS, Superintendencia de Salud CL, Supersalud CO), rating-agency releases (Fitch, AM Best, HR Ratings, PCR Verum, Moody's Local), and 2025–2026 financial press. Every figure below is taken from a retrieved page; where a source is silent (ownership, premiums) that is stated. All amounts in local currency unless marked; USD conversions are approximate and mine.

Macro backdrop that shapes the Mexico pipeline (sourced):
- 2026 Ley de Ingresos ended VAT credit on claims-related third-party costs. AM Best (16 Sep 2026): 40% of insurers with <MXN 1bn premiums posted losses in 2025; roughly half of auto-heavy small insurers lost money; monoline/small carriers face "capitalization pressure". https://news.ambest.com/PR/PressContent.aspx?altsrc=2&refnum=37802
- Fitch (Dec 2025) rates Mexico the only "deteriorating" insurance market in LatAm; combined ratios >100% through 2026; A&H hit hardest. https://www.reinsurancene.ws/fitch-flags-mexico-as-2026-outlier-in-otherwise-steady-latam-insurance-landscape/
- CNSF Q1 2026: sector profit -10.5% y/y; medical-expense line profit -63.5%. https://insurabeat.com/cnsf-mexico-vat-reform-insurer-profits-q1-2026/
- AMIS: gastos médicos segment posted a 6% operating loss in 2025, ~77% of premium paid out in claims, medical inflation ~14%. https://www.cronista.com/mexico/finanzas-economia/crisis-en-seguros-costos-hospitalarios-devoran-la-rentabilidad-y-dejan-perdidas-del-6-en-2025/
- Even GNP: Q1 2026 net profit -91% on a MXN 2,955m VAT "insuficiencia técnica". https://www.cronista.com/mexico/finanzas-economia/golpe-del-iva-tumba-91-la-utilidad-de-gnp-en-el-primer-trimestre/
- Argentina: SSN moved minimum capital to a flat 750,000 UVA (~USD 857k) for all lines, full compliance June 2026; three insurers already in compulsory liquidation. https://www.globalinsurancelaw.com/2026/01/15/regulator-moves-to-prevent-insolvencies-in-argentinas-insurance-market/

## Candidates

### 1. Bupa México, Compañía de Seguros, S.A. de C.V. — Mexico — health insurer (GMM)
- Size: retained premiums USD 344m, capital USD 60m at YE2025; 8th of 47 A&H insurers (Fitch, 2 Oct 2026).
- Signals: 2025 combined ratio 102.3% (vs 96.7% 2022–24 avg) producing a net loss, attributed to VAT reform; Fitch Prism capital score cut to "Somewhat Weak" from "Adequate"; NPW/capital 5.7x (vs 4.6x); sovereign exposure 256% of capital. Rating held at A-/AAA(mex) only via two notches of group support. Fetched 2 Oct 2026: https://www.bmv.com.mx/docs-pub/eventoca/eventoca_1593089_2.pdf
- Ownership: 99.9% Bupa Global Holdings Ltd (Bupa Finance Plc).
- Likely need: USD 20–40m capital top-up to restore Prism to "Adequate" given premium growth; or a quota-share/structured reinsurance solution. Parent-funded unless Bupa decides Mexico is non-core (Bupa is simultaneously absorbing a ~GBP 187m hit in Chile, see #9).
- Why banks avoid: parent-controlled, no debt; the opening is structured capital/reinsurance or a minority partner if Bupa re-prioritises.
- Entry path: Fitch México analysts (report author listed in release); Bupa Global Holdings corporate development; Mexican reinsurance brokers.
- Fit: 3/5 — exact sector and regulatory trigger (VAT + medical inflation) as reference deal, but strong parent makes an outside recap less likely near term.
- Sources: Fitch release above; sector context https://insurabeat.com/cnsf-mexico-vat-reform-insurer-profits-q1-2026/

### 2. Prudential Seguros México, S.A. de C.V. — Mexico — life insurer in sale process
- Size: not in retrieved sources (international IFS BBB, national AAA(mex)).
- Signals: Fitch cut international IFS to BBB from BBB+ and placed both scales on Rating Watch Negative (26 Aug 2026) because parent Prudential Financial Inc. is exiting emerging markets and selling the Mexican unit; standalone profile only BBB-, in line with sovereign; RWN resolution "could take more than six months". https://boletines.latinoinsurance.com/rebaja-fitch-nota-internacional-de-mexicana-prudential-seguros-a-bbb-desde-bbb ; also https://www.slipcase.com/view/fitch-downgrades-prudential-mexicos-international-scale-rating-to-bbb-places-on-rwn (index page only; detail from latinoinsurance)
- Ownership: Prudential Financial, Inc. (NYSE: PRU), US.
- Likely need: secondary / control sale, likely USD 50–100M+ range (unverified; depends on book). Buyer will need to replace parent capital support; a sponsor or regional insurer may want co-investors.
- Why big banks may still be involved: this is a PFI-run process; the angle for a boutique is on the buy-side (family offices / regional insurers needing equity partners) rather than the sell-side.
- Entry path: Fitch México; Mexican life-insurance M&A lawyers (Nader Hayaux Goebel, Creel — see intermediary map).
- Fit: 2/5 — regulated and in play, but a well-advised multinational disposal, not a distressed mid-market owner.

### 3. Agrocapital del Noroeste, S.A. de C.V., SOFOM E.N.R. — Mexico (Los Mochis, Sinaloa) — agri non-bank lender
- Size: loan book MXN 1,019.7m (~USD 55m), equity MXN 214.0m at 1Q26.
- Signals: PCR Verum cut to B/M from BBB/M and 4/M from 3/M, placed on Observación Negativa (23 Jul 2026). NPLs 13.2% (adj 13.8%), reserve coverage 0.3x, cash MXN 1.5m (0.2% of liabilities), single funder (MXN 880.6m) has frozen the line so no new origination; foreclosed assets MXN 35.9m; low-productivity assets 79.1% of equity. https://pcrverum.mx/calificaciones/pcr-verum-baja-calificaciones-de-agrocapital-a-b-m-y-4-m-y-las-coloca-en-observacion-negativa/
- Ownership: founded 2005 as subsidiary of Grupo Ceres (agri group).
- Likely need: USD 10–20m equity/subordinated capital plus refinancing of the single development-bank line; or portfolio sale / wind-down. Classic recap-or-run-off.
- Why banks avoid: single-funder dependence, 13% NPLs, agri concentration (weather, prices), unregulated SOFOM; development bank is the only counterparty.
- Entry path: PCR Verum (rating analyst named in release); Grupo Ceres principals; FIRA/FND-type development-bank workout desks.
- Fit: 4/5 — size, regulatory-adjacent (CNBV-registered ENR), clear capital hole, owner is a family agri group.

### 4. Agropecuaria Financiera, SOFOM E.N.R. — Mexico — sugar-cane lender
- Size: portfolio MXN 564.6m (~USD 30m) at 2Q26, down 17.4% YTD; equity figure not disclosed.
- Signals: PCR Verum cut to BB/M from BB+/M, outlook stays Negative (4 Sep 2026). NPL 18.2% (19.9% adjusted) — highest in company history after two main borrowers defaulted; H1-26 net loss MXN 15.7m; provisions MXN 51.4m; low-productivity assets 103.1% of equity; single funding line restricted to pre-approved operations. https://pcrverum.mx/calificaciones/pcr-verum-baja-a-bb-m-calificacion-de-agropecuaria-financiera-perspectiva-permanece-negativa/
- Ownership: not stated in source.
- Likely need: USD 10–15m recap or sale of the sugar-cane book; company cites a "plan estratégico de saneamiento".
- Why banks avoid: NPL ~20%, equity consumed by DTA/foreclosed assets, mono-crop concentration (53% sugar cane).
- Entry path: PCR Verum; sugar-mill (ingenio) groups that are the ultimate obligors.
- Fit: 4/5 — small end of range but acute, owner-run, and the funder has already pulled back.

### 5. FINDECA, S.A. de C.V., SOFOM E.N.R. — Mexico (Oaxaca) — rural development lender
- Size: portfolio MXN 559.4m (~USD 30m), equity MXN 110.9m at 1Q26.
- Signals: PCR Verum cut to BBB-/M from BBB/M, outlook Negative (20 Aug 2026); HR Ratings has it at HR BB+ (26 Jun 2026). Low-productivity assets 83.1% of equity (other receivables MXN 84.8m from foreclosed-asset sale promises and bridge loans); MXN 64.9m of loans with guarantees drawn; 98.1% of funding from one MXN 600m line; top-15 borrowers 2.7x equity. https://www.bmv.com.mx/docs-pub/eventoca/eventoca_1583349_1.pdf ; HR: https://www.bmv.com.mx/docs-pub/eventoca/eventoca_1570666_2.pdf
- Ownership: not disclosed; 50% independent board.
- Likely need: USD 5–10m fresh equity + diversification of funding; borderline below band but could be bundled with #3/#4 as an agri-SOFOM roll-up.
- Why banks avoid: Oaxaca/Chiapas concentration (~95%), single funder, opaque receivables.
- Entry path: PCR Verum, HR Ratings; development-bank workout teams.
- Fit: 3/5.

## Summary — best three

1. Agrocapital del Noroeste (Mexico, Grupo Ceres): B/M on Observación Negativa, 13% NPLs, single funder frozen, ~USD 55m book — a textbook USD 10–20m recap-or-run-off where no bank will step in; owner is a family agri group with something to protect.
2. Cruz Suiza Seguros (Argentina, Figueiras/Urtubey): regulator-mandated Plan de Regularización with a quantified capital hole, wealthy industrial owners with an expansion plan, and the new 750,000-UVA floor forcing a decision in 2026 — small cheque, high probability of a transaction.
3. Isapre Cruz Blanca / Bupa Chile: the largest Supreme-Court liability in the isapre system (CLP 316.6bn, 12% repaid), loss-making, parent in ICSID arbitration and shrinking the footprint — the clearest "owner wants out, banks won't touch" health-payer exit in LatAm, though above the band and parent-advised.
Honourable mentions: Bupa México (same VAT/medical-inflation mechanics as the reference deal, Prism "Somewhat Weak") and the Patria-owned Vida Tres/Banmédica isapres as co-invest situations.
